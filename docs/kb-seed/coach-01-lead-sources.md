## What a lead is at D2

A lead is any named person who has shown interest in a D2 project and whose contact details
you hold. Interest alone is not a lead — a browser who never gives you a way to reach them is
traffic, not a lead. The moment you have a name and a contact number, you have a lead, and it
becomes your responsibility to record it and follow it up.

Every lead belongs to exactly one agent at a time. That agent is the owner. Ownership matters
because it decides who follows up, who is accountable for the record being accurate, and who
the client will hold responsible for what they were told. If you are unsure who owns a lead,
ask your team leader before contacting them. Two agents calling the same person on the same
day is the fastest way to lose them.

## The four sources you will actually see

**Listing portals.** The lead arrives from a property portal after someone enquires on a
listing you or the agency posted. You usually get a name, a phone number, and the listing they
were looking at. You rarely get anything else — no budget, no timeline, no financing picture.
Portal leads are high volume and low context. Speed matters more here than anywhere else,
because the same person almost always enquired on several listings at once.

**WhatsApp.** Either the client messages the number on a listing or a flyer directly, or a
past client passes your number on. WhatsApp leads arrive with more context than portal leads,
because people tend to say what they want in their first message. They also expect a faster
reply, because they can see you online.

**Walk-ins.** Someone arrives at a sales gallery or an open unit without an appointment. You
get the most context of any source — you can see who came with them, what they react to, and
what they ask about unprompted — and the least time to use it. A walk-in who leaves without
giving you a contact number is a lost lead, no matter how well the conversation went.

**Referrals.** An existing or past client, a colleague, or someone in your own network passes
a name to you. Referrals convert at the highest rate of the four because trust is already
present. They also carry the most risk to your reputation: if you handle a referral badly, you
damage your relationship with the person who referred them as well as with the lead.

## The lifecycle every lead moves through

1. **Captured** — you have a name and a contact number, and you have written them down
   somewhere that is not your own memory.
2. **Contacted** — you have made first contact and it has been received. A message sent and
   never read is not contact.
3. **Qualified** — you know enough to be useful: roughly what they want, roughly what they can
   spend, and roughly when. Qualified does not mean approved for financing; it means you know
   which projects are worth showing them.
4. **Viewing arranged** — a date and place are agreed and confirmed.
5. **Viewed** — the viewing happened, and you have recorded what they responded to.
6. **Negotiating / booking** — they are discussing a specific unit.
7. **Closed won** or **Closed lost** — either the booking is signed, or they have told you no,
   or they have gone quiet past the point where further follow-up is worthwhile.

A lead can go backwards. Someone who cancels a viewing returns to Qualified, not to Closed
lost. Only mark a lead Closed lost when they have said no or when your follow-up cadence has
run its full course without a reply.

## What to record, and why

For every lead: name, contact number, source, the project or listing that generated it, the
date of first contact, and the date and content of the most recent contact. For a qualified
lead, add the budget range they stated, whether they are buying to live in or to invest,
their financing position if they have told you, and their timeline.

Record what the client actually said, not your interpretation of it. "Says around 800k,
mentioned he hasn't spoken to a bank yet" is useful to the next person who reads it. "Serious
buyer" is not.

Never write anything into a lead record that you would not be comfortable with the client
reading. Assume they may one day ask.

## Response time, by source

Portal and WhatsApp leads expect a reply the same day, and realistically within the hour
during working hours. A portal enquirer has usually contacted several agents; the first useful
reply tends to get the conversation. Walk-ins should be recorded the same day while your
memory of the conversation is accurate. Referrals should be contacted the same day out of
courtesy to whoever referred them, even if only to acknowledge and arrange a proper call.

## Common mistakes

Chasing volume over follow-up. Twenty leads contacted once convert worse than five leads
followed up properly.

Qualifying by asking a list of questions in the first message. It reads like a form, and
people stop replying.

Letting a lead go quiet without a final message. A clear, friendly close-out message
occasionally revives a lead months later, and it costs nothing.

Recording nothing after a viewing. What someone reacted to at a viewing is the most valuable
information you will ever collect about them, and it is gone within a day if you do not write
it down.
