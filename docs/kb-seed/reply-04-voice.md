_Starter SOP written so the Reply agent has something to ground a draft on. Derek should
review and correct it before agents send anything based on it._

## What a D2 reply should sound like

Like a competent person texting, not like a company broadcasting.

The agent is sending this from their own phone, under their own name, to someone who may
become a client for years. It has to sound like them.

## Length

Two to four sentences for most replies. If a draft runs past five, something belongs in a
phone call or an attachment instead.

Long messages do not read as thorough. They read as a template.

## Formatting

Plain sentences. No headings, no bullet lists, no bold, no emoji bullets in a WhatsApp reply —
those are the visual signature of a mass message and clients recognise them instantly.

At most one emoji, and only if the client used one first.

Never open with "Dear Sir/Madam" or close with a formal signature block. This is a chat.

## Language

**Mirror the client.** Reply in the language they wrote in. If they mixed English and Malay,
mix it back — that is how people actually speak here, and correcting them into formal English
creates distance.

**Mirror their formality.** "Hi bro, still available?" and "Good afternoon, I would like to
enquire" are two different people and should get two different replies.

Use the client's name if you have it, once, near the start. Not three times.

## What to avoid

**Hype.** "Amazing opportunity", "won't last long", "best deal in Bangsar". It reads as
pressure and it undermines everything factual around it.

**Manufactured urgency.** Scarcity is stated only when it is true and verifiable right now.

**Jargon and abbreviations the client has not used.** VP, MOT, SPA, bumi lot — spell it out or
leave it out. An agent sounds knowledgeable by being clear, not by being opaque.

**Over-apologising.** "So sorry to bother you" invites the reader to agree they were bothered.

**Filler openings.** "Hope you are doing well" adds a line and says nothing.

## What to keep

**One idea per message.** If there are two things to say, the second is usually a follow-up
message, not another paragraph.

**A clear next step.** Every reply should leave the client knowing what happens now — even if
that is only "I'll confirm and come back to you today."

**Honesty about what is not known.** "Let me check and confirm" is a strong sentence. It is
far better than a confident answer that turns out to be wrong.

## The line that must never be crossed

The reply is a draft for the agent to send from their own phone. It must never claim to be
automated, never sign as a bot, and never promise anything the agent has not verified.

Every factual claim in a draft — price, availability, tenure, completion, eligibility, fees —
must come from confirmed project data. If it is not confirmed, the draft says it will be
confirmed. This is not a style rule; it is the difference between a helpful assistant and a
liability.
