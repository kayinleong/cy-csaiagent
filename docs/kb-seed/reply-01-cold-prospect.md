_Starter SOP written so the Reply agent has something to ground a draft on. Derek should
review and correct it before agents send anything based on it._

## When this SOP applies

The inbound is a first message from someone you have not spoken to before. Typically it comes
from a listing portal or from a number on a flyer or listing. It is short, it names a project
or an area, and it asks whether something is available or what the price is.

## What the reply must do

Turn an enquiry into a conversation. That is the whole job. It does not need to close, qualify
fully, or present the project — it needs to earn a second message.

## Structure

1. **Acknowledge and place yourself.** Name the project or listing they asked about. They
   almost certainly enquired on several; do not make them work out who you are.
2. **Answer the question they asked.** If they asked whether it is available, answer that
   first. Deflecting to a question of your own before answering theirs reads as evasive.
3. **Add one fact they did not have.** Tenure, completion status, distance to transit,
   a genuinely notable feature. One, not five.
4. **Ask one open question.** Own stay or investment is the highest-value single question.
5. **Offer a clear next step** that is easy to accept.

## Tone

Warm, direct, and short. Two to four sentences. Write it the way you would say it out loud.

No hard sell in a first reply. No urgency language — "selling fast", "last few units" — unless
it is verifiably true right now, and even then it lands badly on a first contact from a
stranger.

## Rules the draft must not break

- **Never state a price, availability, maintenance fee or completion date that is not
  confirmed in current inventory.** If the fact is not to hand, the draft should say the agent
  will confirm and come back today, rather than guess.
- **Never state bumiputera quota or foreign-buyer eligibility from memory.** These decide
  whether a person can legally buy, and they are project-specific.
- **Do not quote financing terms, interest rates or loan margins.** Those come from a bank, not
  from us.
- **Do not attach a brochure in the first reply.** It is a large unsolicited file and it tends
  to end the conversation.

## Language

Reply in the language the client wrote in. If they wrote in Malay, reply in Malay; if they
mixed English and Malay, mirror the mix rather than correcting it. Match their level of
formality — someone who opens with "Hi bro" should not receive a letter.

## When there is not enough to work with

If the inbound is a bare "Hi" or "Interested" with no project named, the draft should ask
which listing they saw rather than guessing. A wrong guess about which project someone is
asking about is worse than a one-line clarifying question.

## Worked shape

An inbound like *"Hi, is the Bangsar unit still available? What's the price?"* should produce
a reply that: greets and names the project, confirms it will be checked or confirms
availability if known, adds one concrete fact about it, asks whether it is for own stay or
investment, and proposes sending the floor plan or arranging a viewing. Nothing longer.
