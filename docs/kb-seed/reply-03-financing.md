_Starter SOP written so the Reply agent has something to ground a draft on. Derek should
review and correct it before agents send anything based on it. Financing is the highest-risk
category in this knowledge base — read the limits below before using any of it._

## When this SOP applies

The client asks about loans, eligibility, deposits, monthly instalments, interest rates,
approval chances, or what they can afford.

## The hard limit, first

**We are not financial advisers and must not answer as if we were.** Loan eligibility depends
on the client's income, existing commitments, credit record and the bank's own criteria on the
day. None of that is ours to assess, and an agent who estimates it is giving financial advice
they are not licensed to give.

So the draft must never:

- state an interest rate, a margin of finance, or a monthly instalment figure
- estimate how much the client can borrow, or their chance of approval
- comment on their credit record, or suggest ways to improve it
- promise that financing will be arranged or approved
- quote a legal price threshold for foreign buyers, which is state-dependent and changes

If the inbound asks for any of those directly, the reply acknowledges the question, explains
that the bank is the right source, and offers to connect them with the panel financier or with
the agent's team leader. That is a good answer, not a dodge — clients respect being told where
the real answer lives.

## What the reply CAN do

Be genuinely useful within the limit:

- **Explain the process** in general terms: that a loan application follows a booking, that
  banks assess income and existing commitments, that a rough affordability check with a bank
  early on saves time later.
- **Encourage an early check.** The single most useful thing an agent can tell an enquirer is
  to get a rough eligibility check before falling in love with a unit. It is free, it takes
  little time, and it prevents the most painful failure in this business.
- **Offer the panel financier.** If the project has one, offer an introduction.
- **Offer to involve the team leader** for anything beyond general process.
- **State confirmed project facts** — the booking fee, the payment schedule, whether it is
  under construction or completed — provided those come from current inventory and not from
  memory.

## Structure

1. Acknowledge the question directly. Do not open with a disclaimer; it reads as evasive.
2. Give the general, safe part of the answer.
3. Point clearly to the right source — the bank or panel financier.
4. Offer the concrete next step: an introduction, or a call with the team leader.

Three or four sentences.

## Tone

Confident about the process, plain about the limit. There is a large difference between "I
can't advise on that" and "the bank will give you a proper answer on that, and I can put you
in touch today." The second is the same limit, delivered as help.

Do not be apologetic. Clients are not surprised that a property agent does not approve loans.

## The affordability trap

If the client says a budget figure, take it at face value and work within it. Do not do the
arithmetic on what they could stretch to, and do not suggest a more expensive unit on the
basis that financing might cover it. That is the single most common way agents drift into
giving financial advice without realising.

## Escalate rather than guess

Any financing question that cannot be answered inside these limits goes to the team leader or
the panel financier. Escalating is the correct outcome here, not a failure — and it is far
better than a confident answer that turns out to be wrong at the point where the client has
already paid a booking fee.
