_Starter SOP written so the Reply agent has something to ground a draft on. Derek should
review and correct it before agents send anything based on it._

## When this SOP applies

The client has engaged and then raised a reason not to proceed. The common ones are price,
location, timing, size, and comparison with a competing project.

## The principle

An objection is information, not opposition. A client who says "too expensive" has told you
they are still considering it — people do not negotiate about things they have dismissed.

The reply must therefore do two things and in this order: show the objection was heard, then
give one piece of substance that addresses it. Never skip the first half. A reply that jumps
straight to a counter-argument reads as not listening, and it hardens the position.

## Structure

1. **Acknowledge it plainly**, without the word "but". "But" deletes everything before it.
2. **One piece of substance** — a fact, a comparison, or an option. One.
3. **A question or a next step** that keeps the conversation open.

Two to four sentences.

## By objection type

**"Too expensive."** Do not defend the price and do not immediately offer a discount — neither
is yours to do. Establish what it is expensive *relative to*: another project, their budget,
or what they expected for the size. Each needs a different answer. If it is genuinely above
their budget, the useful move is a comparable unit within it, not an argument.

**"I want to wait."** Find out what they are waiting for. A bonus, a sale of another property,
a partner returning from overseas, or general uncertainty. The first three have dates and can
be planned around; the last is usually a different objection wearing a coat.

**"Too far / wrong location."** This one is usually real and not worth arguing with. Ask what
they are measuring the distance to — work, family, a school. Then offer something that
actually fits, if we have it. Trying to talk someone out of a location objection damages trust
and rarely works.

**"Project X is better."** Ask what specifically they preferred. Then be honest about the
comparison, including where the other project genuinely is better. Agents who cannot concede
a single point are not believed on any of them.

**"Need to discuss with my spouse / family."** Almost always genuine. The reply should make it
easy to bring the other person in — offer to send something they can share, or to arrange a
viewing when both can attend. Do not push for a decision from one half of a couple.

## Rules the draft must not break

- **Never invent a discount, rebate, package or incentive.** These are set by the developer
  and change. If the agent believes one exists, they confirm it before it is offered.
- **Never disparage a competing project or another agency.** Compare on facts we can stand
  behind, and concede where the comparison genuinely goes the other way.
- **Never apply pressure or manufacture scarcity.** "Only two units left" is either true and
  verifiable or it is not said.
- **Do not quote financing terms** — margins, rates, approval odds. Those belong to a bank.
- **Do not restate an unverified price** while addressing a price objection.

## Tone

Calm and unhurried. The client is deciding whether they can trust you when the answer is
inconvenient, and that judgement is made here more than anywhere else.

If the objection is fatal — the budget genuinely does not reach, the location genuinely does
not work — say so and offer the alternative. An agent who tells a client honestly that
something is not for them is the agent that client comes back to.
