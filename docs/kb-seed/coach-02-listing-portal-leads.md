_Starter document written to give the Coach something to answer with. Derek should review and
correct every claim before agents rely on it._

## What you are actually dealing with

A portal lead is a person who clicked "enquire" on a listing. Assume three things until proven
otherwise: they enquired on several listings, not just yours; they were looking at the photos
and the price, not the details; and they have not spoken to a bank.

That shapes everything. You are not answering a question — you are trying to be the one agent
out of five who turns a click into a conversation.

## Reply the same hour

Portal leads decay faster than any other source. Someone who enquired an hour ago is still
thinking about property. Someone who enquired yesterday has moved on with their week and is
now being messaged by four other agents.

If you cannot give a proper reply immediately, send a short holding message and follow it with
the real one. A holding message is better than a slow perfect one, but it only buys you a few
hours, so use them.

## The first message

Keep it to four things and nothing else:

1. **Who you are** and which listing they enquired about, so they can place you. They enquired
   on several — do not make them guess which one you are.
2. **One useful fact they did not have.** Something from the listing detail they almost
   certainly did not read: the tenure, the completion status, the walking distance to transit.
   This is what separates you from the agents who send "Hi, are you still interested?"
3. **One open question.** Not a list. One.
4. **A clear next step**, phrased so that the easiest possible reply moves things forward.

Write it as one short paragraph, not a bulleted brochure. It is a message, not a document.

## The one question to ask first

Ask what they are looking for it *for* — to live in, or to rent out. Nothing else changes your
recommendations as much, and almost nobody minds answering it.

Do not open with budget. It reads as a screening question and it invites a defensive answer.
Budget comes up naturally once you are recommending specific units, and the number you get
then is far more honest than the one you get in the first message.

## Qualifying over the next few messages

Work towards knowing four things, in roughly this order, and get them across a conversation
rather than an interrogation:

- **Purpose** — own stay or investment.
- **Area** — whether they are fixed on this location or open to nearby alternatives.
- **Timeline** — buying now, or looking ahead six to twelve months. This changes which
  projects make sense far more than most agents expect.
- **Financing position** — whether they have checked their loan eligibility yet. Most portal
  enquirers have not, and saying so is normal and not embarrassing.

If they will not answer any of these, that is information too. It usually means they are early
and browsing, which is fine — put them on a slower follow-up cadence rather than dropping
them.

## What not to do

Do not send the full brochure in the first message. It is a large file to an unknown number
and it ends the conversation rather than starting one.

Do not quote a price the listing does not already show, and never quote one you have not
verified against current inventory. Prices and availability change, and a number you got wrong
in the first message follows you all the way to the booking conversation.

Do not promise availability of a specific unit before you have checked. "Let me confirm what
is still available and come back to you today" is a stronger message than a confident answer
that turns out to be wrong.

Do not push for a viewing in the first reply if they have not told you what they want. A
viewing booked on no information is usually a viewing that gets cancelled.

## When they do not reply

Follow up twice, then stop and close out. A reasonable pattern is: the next day, then about a
week later, then a short final message making it easy for them to come back later. Each
follow-up should carry something new — a genuine update, a comparable unit, a change in
availability — not just "just checking in".

Three unanswered messages is your signal to move on. Mark the lead closed and record why, so
that if they resurface in six months the next conversation starts from what you already know.
